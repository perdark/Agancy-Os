# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The primary user is the agency owner preparing for a first prospect meeting.
They work as a single operator, often with little time and imperfect source
material: a business identity, a real logo, available public evidence, and one
useful context sentence. Their primary task is to turn that material into a
specific, polished prototype they can confidently present.

## Product Purpose

Agency OS is a first-meeting prototype engine. It helps the operator understand
a prospect, generate candidate directions, judge the rendered artifacts, and
present the strongest truthful mockup. Success is a stored, independently
checked, meeting-ready artifact that demonstrates understanding and improves
the chance of winning the deal; prompts and strategy are supporting material,
not the value event. This is Engine 1 of the long-term engine roadmap in
`docs/VISION.md`; the later engines wait until Engine 1 wins real meetings.

## Brand Personality

Prepared, specific, and honest. The product should feel quietly premium and
decisive without overstating confidence. Its language separates verified facts,
operator statements, and hypotheses, and always makes the next useful action
clear.

## Anti-references

- Generic category templates that could belong to any prospect.
- Dashboard-first or architecture-first screens that bury the mockup beneath
  workflow mechanics and internal cards.
- Client-facing views that expose model errors, stage contracts, confidence
  machinery, or implementation diagnostics.
- Polished visuals that disguise invented prices, products, hours, locations,
  audience claims, or commerce rules.
- Long prompt-library experiences that add ceremony without improving the
  rendered artifact.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with the artifact and the operator's next action.
2. Make minimal, imperfect input safe; unknown information is valid input.
3. Keep every important claim traceable or visibly marked as a hypothesis.
4. Preserve the thin successful workflow and add rules only when evidence
   proves they improve repeatability.
5. Separate the operator workspace from the client-safe presentation surface.
6. Treat independent rendered-artifact verification as part of generation.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Accessibility, contrast, and legibility are release gates. Desktop and mobile
must be complete and free of overflow or blank states. Arabic and English must
use correct direction, typography, digit handling, and language-specific
spacing. Controls need visible focus, keyboard access, clear error/status copy,
and reduced-motion-safe state feedback.
